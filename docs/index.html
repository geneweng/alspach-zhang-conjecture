Open problem · Algebraic graph theory

Are there Cayley snarks?

A working project to settle the Alspach–Zhang conjecture: either prove that every cubic Cayley graph is 3-edge-colourable, or construct one that is not.

Conjecture (Alspach–Zhang, Louisville 1992)

Every connected cubic Cayley graph is 3-edge-colourable.

Equivalently: every Cayley graph of valency at least 2 admits a nowhere-zero 4-flow. A cubic Cayley graph that is not 3-edge-colourable is called a Cayley snark (Nedela–Škoviera). None is known.

Why this problem

Tait showed in 1880 that the Four Colour Theorem is the statement that every bridgeless planar cubic graph is 3-edge-colourable. Drop planarity and it fails: the Petersen graph is the smallest counterexample, and the non-trivial ones are called snarks. Every known snark, and every known non-hamiltonian vertex-transitive graph, fails to be a Cayley graph. The conjecture says this is not a coincidence: the regular action of a group on the vertices forces a Tait colouring.

The conjecture is implied by the folklore conjecture that every Cayley graph has a Hamilton cycle (cubic Cayley graphs have even order). Before the cycle double cover theorem was proved in 2026, it also implied that formerly open statement for cubic Cayley graphs. The new proof supplies a useful eight-point palette reformulation of this problem, but does not by itself force a Tait colouring. A generator-separated version reduces the missing step to a four-by-four Hall condition; twelve of its twenty-four matching cases remain affine linear. It remains a natural, sharply posed test case for the general belief that symmetry excludes snarks.

What is known

Write X(G; a, x) for the Cayley graph of G with respect to {a, x, x−1}, where a is an involution and x has odd order. Every other cubic Cayley graph is trivially colourable, so the conjecture is about these graphs only.

What a counterexample must look like

Wanted: a Cayley snark
  1. A group G = ⟨a, x⟩ with a² = 1 and x of odd order; the graph is X(G; a, x). Three-involution generating sets and even-order x never work.
  2. If it is a smallest one: G is non-abelian simple, or G has exactly one non-trivial proper normal subgroup T, of index 2, with T simple or T = S × S (Nedela–Škoviera). Any other Cayley snark covers one of these.
  3. More than 352 439 vertices, and none of the 85 groups verified so far. The first unverified admissible groups are PSL(2,89), S9, PSL(2,97), …
  4. Girth 3 only if x has order 3; girth 5 only if x has order 5; never girth 4. In the order-3 case the graph is the truncation of a cubic graph with an arc-regular automorphism group, and a smallest counterexample must have a non-abelian simple arc-regular group.
  5. Its x-cycles form an odd 2-factor; every other 2-factor must also contain an odd cycle. A Hamilton cycle would already colour it, so it is also a non-hamiltonian Cayley graph, which would refute the hamiltonicity folklore conjecture.

The plan

Updated 6 September 2026: a joint polynomial criterion now combines the projective label recurrence with the common-translation chord equations. It gives exact realizability and simultaneous-unit-gain tests, a one-row certificate for the fixed q=2048 diagram without full rank, and a forbidden anchored three-chord pattern valid over every characteristic-two field. A new negative control shows why the recurrence alone is insufficient. A uniform exclusion of all realizable gain-one obstructions remains unproved. The best proved asymptotic full-cycle coverage remains at least 13/16. The research review and follow-up separate proofs, finite evidence and remaining obstacles. The full conjecture remains open.

Track A · Prove it

  • Joint polynomial elimination and a forbidden anchored pattern. Polynomial equations from the label recurrence and the common chord translation exactly identify realizable diagrams with simultaneous unit root gains. A Bezout identity proves that chords {1,3}, {2,7}, {4,6} cannot coexist in the projective ordering, over any characteristic-two field. One three-chord row certifies the fixed q=2048 diagram without full rank. Six stored diagrams pass the polynomial test, with twelve independently audited partner lifts. A uniform theorem excluding every realizable obstruction remains missing; neither coverage nor global bounds increase.
  • Unrestricted temporary colours and exact recolouring closures. The enlarged seven-change families have full rank in the three recorded deficient cases at (q,t,c)=(1024,49,80), (1024,287,683), and (2048,343,1766), with independently checked minors. For the restricted q=2048 family, eight kernel witnesses prove minimum rational support three; a three-chord root path nevertheless has successful gain 178. Combined ordinary and seven-change moves reach all 570 three-colourings on 96 small parameters. On 74 of those parameters, even the all-colouring root matrix fails the full-rank test, despite successful gains. Full rank is sufficient, not necessary; general reachability and arithmetic nonvanishing remain unproved.
  • A genuine limit of the fixed-family lattice target, and a one-prime replacement. At q=2048,t=343,c=1766, the chord-seeded seven-change family has 425 independent rows on 512 coordinates. Kernel witnesses rule out every nonzero coordinate multiple, disproving the proposed uniform inclusion even in the no-square residual class. The chord gains nevertheless generate the full multiplicative group, so full rank modulo any one prime divisor of q−1 is sufficient. Nine of ten stored q=2048 cases and all ten stored q=4096 cases pass the restricted test; an ordinary Kempe prefix restores full rank in the exceptional case, as does the enlarged seed family above. These are finite certificates, not a universal move bound or new exhaustive colouring coverage.
  • Collective nonvanishing through signed root-path lattices. An integer combination equal to twice one chord coordinate forces some candidate root gain to be non-unit, since the chord gains have odd order. All 51 stored folded diagrams (942 weighted canonical failures through q=1024) pass this sufficient test. Full rank and power-of-two index are too strong for all canonical failures, although all ten stored residual diagrams satisfy them. A genuine non-natural q=128 colouring has both gains one, so changing the colouring alone is insufficient. A uniform lattice inclusion remains unproved; no global bound or exhaustive colouring coverage increases.
  • Improved proved density: at least 13/16 asymptotically. In the residual full-cycle class Tr(1/c)=Tr(1/(c+t))=1, a unique two-chord alternating exchange exists exactly when Tr(1/[c(c+t)])=0. It colours at least one of c,c+t; a non-spanning complement can suffice. Combined with the original trace test, an exact character-sum count guarantees at least (13q−23−10√q)/16 successful translations out of q−2, uniformly for every full-cycle t. The criterion is audited through q=4096. A q=2048 example where both the canonical matching and the square fail prevents dropping the paired qualification. The full conjecture remains open.
  • Exact short-exchange obstruction and full circuit certificates. Two further trace tests, with an explicit cubic correction, count all three-chord alternating circuits in the non-pentagon residual class. An asymptotic 1/32 parameter subfamily has no alternating circuit of one, two or three chords; the proved lower bound is positive for every full-cycle t once q≥16384. A new affine-voltage criterion is necessary and sufficient for any folded matching to lift with an even complement. A q=128 control shows that a three-chord exchange can fail for both translations despite non-unit root gain. These results motivate longer or global matching changes; they do not raise the proved colouring density or resolve the full conjecture.
  • A target-free seven-change escape, using two temporary edges. In the rigid q=256 example, one temporary-colour edge cannot escape the unsuccessful three-colour Kempe class, while two suffice in seven verified changes. A four-endpoint pairing theorem characterises return to three colours; non-unit gain still needs checking. The deterministic rule repairs all 942 stored weighted canonical failures through q=1024, all 20 stored larger controls, and the sole canonical-failure pair among all 8190 generating translations for q=8192,t=13. This last result concerns one trace only. Seven changes may involve long paths; no uniform bounded-chord or successful-pivot theorem is claimed.
  • Current target: Sylow-2 identity avoidance. In a quotient by a 2-subgroup P, a lifted matching has exactly |P|B odd circuits, where B counts odd quotient circuits with identity word. All 358 tested PSL(2,q) pair orbits have a successful matching; two quotients at q=17 are not themselves colourable. Exact counting in a structurally specified minimum-cost class gives E B < 1 on 65 representatives, exhausting 286 863 matchings. Nonemptiness and the inequality on an infinite family remain unproved.
  • Proved positive-density family. For every q=2m ≥ 4, full-cycle x(z)=1/(z+t), and a(z)=z+c with c not 0 or t, the Cayley graph on PSL(2,q) colours if Tr(1/c)=0 or Tr(1/(c+t))=0. An explicit matching leaves only circuits of length 2(q+1). For each full-cycle t, this covers at least half the generating translations and asymptotically three quarters, by an exact Kloosterman-sum count. This is a sufficient family, not a percentage of the full conjecture resolved. The earlier pentagon families c=1,t+1 remain valid.
  • Current construction target: the residual trace class. Reversal folds the matching problem to a path with chords on q/2 vertices. A proved scalar-product formula computes the distinguished circuit word. All 942 canonical failures among 531 468 normalized parameters through q=1024 have a computationally certified root-contained, non-unit-gain repair. Starting from all chords instead gives the new trace-test and crossing-square constructions: together they handle 824 of those failures, leaving 118 outside these short exchanges. Root containment alone is insufficient, and even unrestricted matchings fail the sufficient target in a reordered-label control. Uniform existence for the remaining projective parameters, with both inverse traces equal to 1, is still open.
  • Three-chord nonvanishing has a quartic criterion. Apart from the two already-colourable pentagon families, at most eight translations per full-cycle t can admit a spanning three-chord exchange with unit gain. The proof uses exact short-word polynomial identities; genuine exceptions occur at q=16 and q=1024. All 118 stored residual failures through q=1024 have successful spanning exchanges along a single alternating circuit, with three chords in 72 cases and five in the other 46. The later test below disproves extending that five-chord spanning bound to every q.
  • The five-chord spanning bound fails at q=4096. For t=681, c=1207 modulo θ12+θ3+1, all six single alternating cycles with at most five chords leave non-spanning paths. Independent graph and fixed-point-word enumerations agree. A six-chord exchange spans and colours; a four-chord exchange also colours because its extra quotient circuit is even. The survey gives an off-path sheet-parity criterion and applies classical endpoint-rotation parity to obtain another spanning path, without a size bound. Non-unit gain is not automatic. These are revised proof targets, not a proof of the conjecture.
  • Full-cycle finite coverage reaches q=2048. The proved trace criterion and a complete residual audit certify all 1 395 372 normalized generating parameters with x of order 2049. The q=4096 scan is partial. Other generator orders are not covered and the global smallest-counterexample bound is unchanged.
  • A second symmetric-group family is now proved: if N is odd, x is an N-cycle, a has r disjoint transpositions, and ⟨a,x⟩ = SN, then the Cayley graph is 3-edge-colourable whenever r ≤ 7, N ≥ 4r+1, (r,N)=(9,19), or N=23. Point- and two-set-stabilizer certificates settle every finite boundary through seven transpositions. At the first nine-transposition boundary, adaptive point-quotient matchings and exact monodromy orders certify all 17!! = 34 459 425 labelled involutions. In degree 23, the quotient by A22 is a 46-vertex cycle permutation graph, and the exhaustive Goedgebeur–Renders–Van Overberghe census proves it colourable; this also settles eleven transpositions.
  • The transposition case is now proved for every symmetric group: if N ≥ 3, a is a transposition, x has odd order and ⟨a,x⟩ = SN, then the cubic Cayley graph is 3-edge-colourable. In even degree x has two coprime odd cycles; in odd degree it is one N-cycle and the transposition endpoints have coprime cyclic distance. Explicit matchings in the two-subset quotient leave only paths capped by semi-edges, apart from one harmless even quotient circuit at (N,k)=(5,2). The remaining full-cycle target starts with nine transpositions in degrees 21 through 35, together with odd-order generators having several cycles.
  • A uniform monodromy construction now handles the specified family G = Ss+1, x an odd s-cycle and a the transposition joining its fixed point to the cycle, for every odd s ≥ 5. Every centraliser-invariant minimum-face matching has an even complement. The proof allows odd pentagons in the two-subset quotient: their traversal words have even order s−1, so their lifts are even. Exhaustive quotient checks reach s = 11, with an independent full S6 check. The remaining challenge is to control these word orders for more general generating pairs; the full conjecture remains open.
  • The signed-loop count now satisfies a congruence using the regular group action: for any invariant matching family, its unsigned loop count U and Tait-colouring count T agree modulo 4|G|2 when 4 divides |G|. A nonzero unsigned residue certifies a colouring. Equivalently, a matching with at most r+1 complementary circuits is certified if 2r is the largest power of two dividing its stabiliser order. Exact orbit checks give certificates on A5, S5 and the order-80 affine example, including non-Hamiltonian complements in the latter two. No invariant subfamily of the order-50 face passes this test. The remaining target is to prove the sufficient bound in the non-solvable cases, or to obtain information beyond this modulus.
  • Generators of order 3: it now suffices to show that every cubic graph with a non-abelian simple arc-regular automorphism group is 3-edge-colourable. The general statement is true up to 10 000 vertices by Conder's census.
  • The remaining index-2 case: bi-Cayley graphs B(T; x, α(x)) with x of odd order at least 5. Find a consecutive 2-factor in the cyclically ordered bipartite coset-incidence graph from survey §4; this would give a colouring. Start with T = An, α a transposition.
  • Look for an H-invariant colouring for a large subgroup H avoiding the conjugacy class of x (the quotient is a pregraph with semi-edges; survey §4). In every computed case one exists for a subgroup of index at most 9240, often a point stabiliser or an involution centraliser. A general construction would prove the conjecture; the parity obstruction of §4 shows the largest admissible subgroup is not always the right one.
  • Use the reformulation of §4: a colouring is an a-invariant function G → Z3 whose restriction to every x-cycle is "admissible" (a simple local rule on where the value changes). For x of order 5 this means: constant on three consecutive vertices, the other two colours on the remaining two.
  • Extend the Cayley-map methods of Glover–Kutnar–Malnič–Marušič from (ax)³ = 1 to (ax)t = 1 for small t.
  • Exploit the 2026 cycle-double-cover construction: its eight-point palette leads to a symmetric quotient form and, for order-five generators, a signed-cycle colouring criterion. A quotient perfect matching guarantees a locally valid start. Although single translated-circuit repairs work on all 4090 obstructed A5 supports, the universal rule is now disproved: an explicit PSL(2,11) matching has 168 distinct single flips, all locally invalid. Two simultaneous flips colour it. A second example shows that inclusion-minimal valid blocks need not improve. The survey proves an exact implication criterion for valid combinations of flips; all 561 obstructed starts in a 6144-matching PSL(2,11) sample reach colourings using these more flexible moves. Proving that a suitable closed combination always decreases oddness remains open. These counterexamples concern repair rules, not the Alspach–Zhang conjecture.
  • For order-five generators, restrict to the minimum-a-edge matching face: exactly one selected a-edge per x-cycle. The survey proves that this nonempty class is closed under all translated circuit flips and has exactly the quotient's matching exchanges. The quotient is an orientably regular map whose truncation is the original Cayley graph; the desired colouring is a nowhere-zero F22-flow with a consecutive multiplicity-three value. Every such flow is a face-boundary flow plus a surface-homology class. A defect-incidence multigraph and a two-sheeted interlacement formula track oddness. The number of fixed-colour solutions in the face is now an exact signed-loop state sum, equivalently a three-state local tensor with a G-orbit expansion; proving that this sum is nonzero is the new symmetry-sensitive target. Two independent enumerators exhaust 4336 centralizer-invariant restricted matchings on twelve specified PSL(2,11) pairs; all 430 obstructed starts colour in at most three single flips, and all 3563 obstructed starts in a separate 6144-matching unrestricted sample have an immediate strict repair. However, an explicit restricted matching refutes two natural uniform negative-drift arguments, and four genus-70 PSL(2,11) maps have 5-chromatic duals, so the zero homology class is not enough. The outstanding target is an even complement in this face, not a claim that random repair must improve or that a Hamiltonian complement must exist there.

Track B · Find a Cayley snark

  • Exhaustive search over the admissible groups in order of size, using the SAT-based checker in the repository (quotients by large subgroups keep the instances tiny: the largest quotient solved so far has 9240 vertices for groups of order up to 300 696). Next in line: PSL(2,89), S9, PSL(2,97).
  • Targeted families where the quotient trick is weakest: PSL(2,q) with x of order p and few odd-order subgroups; a full cycle in Sn with nine transpositions in degree 21 and odd degrees 25 through 35. The current combinatorial target is the three-fixed-point involution path question recorded in the survey.
  • Bi-Cayley graphs of simple groups built directly, without the group of order 2|T|, to reach larger T.
  • Measure oddness and resistance of near-misses: which cubic Cayley graphs need many Kempe swaps to colour?
